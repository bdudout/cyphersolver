# Ridolfi to Piero de' Medici, Milan, October 1494: reading

Giovan Battista Ridolfi, Florentine envoy at the Milanese court, to Piero di Lorenzo de' Medici: three letters with cipher passages, Archivio di Stato di Firenze, Mediceo avanti il Principato (MAP) LXXIV docs 112 (16 October), 113 (21 October) and 115 (30 October 1494). Key: the Milan embassy key sheet MAP LXXII 183, plus signs not on the sheet (see [README.md](README.md) and [key.json](key.json)).

This is a partial reading. Every cipher word has a proposed reading except two groups in doc 113, but only about a third of the words are free of a choice between sign values, and a blind test on a deciphered sibling letter got 28 of 38 words right. Treat the connected text as a proposal whose secure parts are the words graded H or C in [reading.txt](reading.txt).

How to read the text below:

- Cipher passages are in *italics*; everything else is the clear hand.
- Spelling of the cipher passages is normalized for u/v and for doubled consonants (the cipher writes *castelo*, *quelo*, *Isabela*). Letters that no cipher sign stands for are in [brackets]: abbreviations (*pr[efa]ta*, *M[aes]tà*) and letters Ridolfi left out (*Pav[i]a*, *G[i]ovanni*). The literal key output of every word is in [reading.txt](reading.txt).
- *[il Re]* and *[Duca o Lodovico]* stand for two person signs that are not on the key sheet and are not glossed anywhere seen; their identities come from context. **[?]** is an unread group; (?) marks a doubtful reading, in the cipher or in the clear hand.
- The clear text was read from the same 1272 px images and has not been collated.

## Doc 112, c. 256r, Milan, 16 October 1494

Mag[nifi]ce maior honor[ande]. Adì xiij scripsi l'ultima ad V. M[agnificen]tia. Non ho vostre. Et per questa mi resta poco a dire altro che quanto vedrete che scrivo a' S[igno]ri Octo: et non per altra cagione che perché intendiate il procedere innanzi del Christ[ianissi]mo Re spaccio questo cavallaro.

*Intendo che arrivando in Pav[i]a*, quando *[il Re] fu entrato in castello, la duchessa Bona* si li fece incontro *in certa sala, dove fu la prima visitatione; et di quivi* dipoi(?) smontando(?) *a la camera del [Duca o Lodovico]*, che pur fussi *ne letto malato*, altro particular non s'intese.

*Ne' g[i]orni innanzi* dicono che, sendo ito *a Pavia lo edicto(?) ha fatto di fare ricomperare il dazio de la macine, quelli cittadini si ristrinsono insieme et* mandorono *al [Duca o Lodovico] a dolersi di tale gravezza, et che* non potevano supportare. Dicono *[Duca o Lodovico] rispose loro* che quando *non la pagassino, non ne li strignerebbe(?); che facessino quello che pareva loro, che non diceva che pagassino né che non pagassino.* Credo però che *le poste siano legate in modo che* di questo sia *da farne poco caso.*

Voi vedrete quanto scrivo a' S[igno]ri Octo del mio soprastar qui. Et oltre alla cagione *dico per quella, non vorrei che qualchuno si pigliassi piacere d'ogni sinistro o caso che ne avenissi, che* per quanto comprendo *è di qualità da non li manchare scusa; et chi havessi male, saria suo danno.* Ad V. M. mi racomando, quale Dio felice conservi. M[edio]l[an]i die xvi octobris 1494. Jo. Baptista de Ridolphis or[ator].

### Translation

Magnificent and most honoured sir. On the 13th I wrote my last to Your Magnificence. I have nothing from you. In this letter there is little left for me to say beyond what you will see I write to the Otto; I send this courier only so that you know of the Most Christian King's advance.

I hear that on arriving in Pavia, when [the King] had entered the castle, the Duchess Bona came to meet him in a certain hall, where the first greeting took place; and from there, then(?) dismounting(?) at the chamber of [the Duke or Lodovico], who was indeed ill in bed, nothing further in particular was learned.

In the days before, they say, the edict(?) having gone to Pavia that he has had made to have the milling duty (dazio della macina) redeemed, those citizens drew together and sent to [the Duke or Lodovico] to complain of such a burden, and that they could not bear it. They say [the Duke or Lodovico] answered them that if they did not pay it he would not force them(?); that they should do as they saw fit, that he was not telling them either to pay or not to pay. I believe, however, that the stakes are so tied up that little is to be made of this.

You will see what I write to the Otto about my staying on here. Beyond the reason I give in that letter, I would not want anyone to take pleasure in any mishap or accident that might come of it, since as far as I understand he is the sort who will not lack an excuse; and whoever came to harm, the loss would be his own. I commend myself to Your Magnificence, whom God keep in happiness. Milan, 16 October 1494. Giovan Battista Ridolfi, envoy.

Notes. (1) The two person signs (a long-tailed 2 with a raised letter) are not on the key sheet, and no glossed instance of them has been found: the similar-looking group in doc 110 c.250r is a plain 2 (b) with raised cipher letters, glossed "B[ernar]do", i.e. an abbreviated name. "[il Re]" (raised a-sign) rests on four contexts across the three letters: entered the castle; "sua Maestà" in the next clause of 113; "apresentarsi al"; "a la corte di". It could equally stand for "Sua Maestà". The second sign (raised d-or-l sign) is the Duke if the sick man in bed is meant (Gian Galeazzo was dying at Pavia) and Lodovico if the raised letter is l and the answer to the citizens is his; the same sign is used in all three places, so one of the two fits less well somewhere. It is left open here. (2) "lo edicto ha fatto" is the weakest clause of the letter: the letters fit, the syntax is rough, and who "ha fatto" is not stated. (3) The last sentence of block 2 reads letter for letter "le poste siano legate in modo che ... da farne poco caso"; the sense given is a proposal.

## Doc 113, c. 257r-v, Milan, 21 October 1494

Magnifice maior honor[ande] etc. Sendo venuto publica fama questa mattina qui in Milano essere morto lo Ill[ustrissi]mo S[ignor] Duca di Milano a Pavia stanotte alle viiij hore, come scrivo a' S[igno]ri Octo di Pratica: perché ne habbiate notitia rimando questo cavallaro con ogni celerità li sarà possibile. Qui non appare sino ad hora alchuna dimostratione. Et è da credere delle cose di questo stato non habbia a innovare cosa alchuna. La Duchessa M[adon]na Bona sino sabbato tornò qui in Milano. El male del S[igno]re dicevano essere febre con dolori colici: et dipoi è sopragiunto lassatione di ventre.

Non havendo vostre l[ette]re, né sentendo molto di nuovo di qua, non so che dirmi altro. Èssi decto essere stato preso a Cesena, o vero a Faenza, uno Bianchino capo di cavalli leggieri di questo S[igno]re, et comprehendesi debba pure essere cosa d'importantia. Tandem si dà fuoca(?) per spillo(?): come interviene delle cose che non piacciono. Dovete intenderlo meglio che cosa la sia: et io non vene posso dire altro.

Alcontinuo(?) riscaldano(?) per quello s'intende le preparationi di mandare a' danni vostri: se già la morte del Duca non li farà mutare proposito. Fate ogni opera che Franzesi non vi si allogino in casa: che non li possendo comportare gl'amici in casa di chi e' sono al presente, è da considerare quanto e' sarebbono insopportabili ne' luoghi che e' riputassino inimici.

*Intendo che quando [il Re] fu a Pavia, la sera di notte a hore* iij(?) *fu con la duchessa Bona per presso a due hore sua M[aes]tà sola con el figl[i]uolo del conte di Sam Polo, che è nipote de la pr[efa]ta duchessa Bona. La duchessa Isabella dice non vuole a[p]resentarsi al [Re],* anchora che *la ne fussi richiesta, pigl[i]ando scusa di sentirsi indisposta.* Altro non *s'intende.*

Questo cavallaro, che è Camprianino(?), *dice che in camino tra Parma et il Borgho a San Donnino trovò, vestito a uso di cavalaro, fra Lauro; et dice li quello intenderete* **[?] [?]** *da lui a bocca. Lorenzo et G[i]ovanni di Pierfranc[esc]o dice erono a Modona.* Né altro. Ad V. M. mi racomando, la quale Dio felice conservi. Mediolani die xxi octobris 1494.

Tenuta a hore xviiij, et di nuovo ho mandato da m[esser] B. Calcho: rafferma la morte esser vera, et che non ha havuto l[ette]re. Jo. Bap[tis]ta de Ridolphis or[ator].

### Translation

Magnificent and most honoured sir. Public report having come this morning here in Milan that the Most Illustrious Lord Duke of Milan died at Pavia last night at the ninth hour, as I write to the Otto di Pratica, I send this courier back with all the speed he can manage so that you have notice of it. So far no demonstration is to be seen here, and it is to be believed that nothing in the affairs of this state will change. The Duchess Madonna Bona came back here to Milan as long ago as Saturday. The Duke's illness was said to be fever with colic pains; then a flux of the bowels came on.

Having no letters from you and hearing little news here, I do not know what else to say. It has been said that one Bianchino, a captain of light horse of this Lord, was taken at Cesena or at Faenza, and it is understood it must be a matter of some weight. In the end (the clause "si dà fuoca per spillo" is not securely read), as happens with things that displease. You must know better what it is; I can tell you no more.

They keep heating up(?), from what one hears, the preparations to send against you, unless the Duke's death makes them change their purpose. Do everything to keep the French from lodging in your house: if their friends, in whose house they now are, cannot put up with them, consider how unbearable they would be in places they held to be enemy.

I hear that when [the King] was at Pavia, in the evening after dark at the third(?) hour, His Majesty was with the Duchess Bona for close to two hours, alone with the son of the Count of Saint-Pol, who is nephew of the said Duchess Bona. The Duchess Isabella, they say, is not willing to present herself to [the King], although she was asked to, excusing herself as feeling unwell. Nothing else is heard.

This courier, who is Camprianino(?), says that on the road between Parma and Borgo San Donnino he met Fra Lauro, dressed as a courier; and he says he told him what you will hear [two groups unread] from him by word of mouth. Lorenzo and Giovanni di Pierfrancesco, he says, were at Modena. Nothing else. I commend myself to Your Magnificence, whom God keep in happiness. Milan, 21 October 1494.

Held until the 19th hour; I sent again to messer B. Calco: he confirms the death is true, and that he has had no letters. Giovan Battista Ridolfi, envoy.

Notes. (1) The hour numeral before "fu con" is in clear and is small; iij is the likeliest reading. (2) The son of the Count of Saint-Pol is Louis de Luxembourg, count of Ligny, whose mother was a sister of Bona of Savoy; this identification has not been verified against a reference work, and the cipher gives only "nipote de la pr[efa]ta duchessa Bona". (3) "Camprianino" and the two clear-text phrases marked (?) are doubtful readings of the clear hand, not of the cipher.

## Doc 115, c. 263r, Milan, 30 October 1494

Mag[nifi]ce maior honor[ande] etc. Adì xxv vi scripsi l'ultima per quanto sino allora intendevo. Dipoi hebbi la vostra de' xxiiij, alla quale accade breve risposta. Et questo cavallaro mando perché habbiate notitia come il Duca di Ferrara s'aspecta domani qui, come vedrete per la l[ette]ra scrivo a' S[igno]ri Octo di Pratica. Et questo Ill[ustrissi]mo S[igno]re doverrà venir innanzi col Christ[ianissi]mo Re alla impresa vostra: *di che si fa opinione di qua non habbiate a potere resistere, per venire con grandi forze, et darsi a 'ntendere che costì ne la città habbia a essere dissensione.* Et poi che costoro procedono di venire in Lunigiana con tanto num[er]o di cavalli, debbono essere in disegno non vi havere a soprastar molto, *et darvi faccenda di più luoghi.*

Così s'è decto la M[adon]na di Furlì essere accordata con loro, *et che gl'[h]anno promesso Faenza.* Dio sia quello che vi facci deliberare il meglio. *Lor[en]zo et G[i]ovanni si dice sono a la corte di [il Re].* Dallo or[ator] vostro lì doverrete haver il tutto. Né altro mi occorre per questa se non racomandarmi alla vostra Mag[nificen]tia, che Dio conservi felice. M[edio]l[an]i die xxx octobris 1494. Jo. Baptista de Ridolphis or[ator].

### Translation

Magnificent and most honoured sir. On the 25th I wrote you my last, with what I knew up to then. Since then I have had yours of the 24th, which needs only a short answer. I send this courier so that you know the Duke of Ferrara is expected here tomorrow, as you will see from the letter I write to the Otto di Pratica. And this Most Illustrious Lord is to go forward with the Most Christian King on the enterprise against you: of which the opinion here is that you will not be able to resist, since they come with great forces and are persuaded that there in the city there will be dissension. And since they are proceeding to come into Lunigiana with so great a number of horse, they must be planning not to stay there long, and to give you trouble in several places.

It has likewise been said that the Lady of Forlì has come to terms with them, and that they have promised her Faenza. God be the one who makes you decide for the best. Lorenzo and Giovanni are said to be at the court of [the King]. From your envoy there you should have the whole of it. Nothing else occurs to me for this letter except to commend myself to Your Magnificence, whom God keep in happiness. Milan, 30 October 1494. Giovan Battista Ridolfi, envoy.

Notes. (1) "alla impresa vostra" could be "nostra"; the abbreviation is the same stroke. (2) "gl'hanno promesso Faenza" is written g-l-a-nn-o; the object pronoun is not marked for gender.
