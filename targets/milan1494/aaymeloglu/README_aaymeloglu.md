# Florentine orators at the Milanese court, 1494: Ridolfi and Alamanni

**Status: partial, 13 November 2026.** Two letters in the same Florentine embassy cipher, read with a key sheet that survives in the same archive. Every reading below is a proposal at the stated grade; neither letter is read to the point where every word is secure.

## What the letters say

**Ridolfi, October 1494**, from Milan as Charles VIII came through Lombardy. 16 October: the King's arrival at Pavia castle, received by the Duchess Bona; a person written with an unglossed sign (the Duke or Lodovico) "ill in bed"; the Pavia citizens' protest against the milling duty, and the answer that they should neither pay nor not pay. 21 October, the day Gian Galeazzo Sforza's death was announced: the King spent about two hours at night with Bona, alone except for the son of the Count of Saint-Pol, "nipote de la pr[efa]ta duchessa"; Duchess Isabella would not present herself, pleading illness; fra Lauro met on the road dressed as a courier; Lorenzo and Giovanni di Pierfrancesco at Modena. 30 October: opinion in Milan that Florence cannot resist the French force and that they count on dissension in the city; Forlì's accord and the promise of Faenza; the two Pierfrancesco brothers at the King's court.

**Alamanni, April 1494**, from Lodovico Sforza's court at Vigevano. Alamanni privately urges Piero that the King of Naples's plan to "voltare Genova" is "l'unica medicina alla salute di tutto questo male", that it must not be delayed and must be entered only with preparation enough to go "a manifesta vittoria", with Florence helping "sotto mante[?]o" (sense: mantello); that while Genoa stays as it is, doing it cleanly would be "la salute del re Alfonso et n[ost]ra"; that name number 22 (probably Lodovico) will never trust the King; and that Piero must tell no one, so that Alamanni is not charged with wanting war "per mettere la città in travagl[i]o". On 221v Lodovico's three threats: Genoa; France (unread); closing the road of the Florentine merchants in the West.

## The key

The cipher is the alphabet on **MAP LXXII doc. 183** (recto image [79536](https://archiviodistatofirenze.cultura.gov.it/map/riproduzione/?id=79536); the verso is endorsed "Cifra con Paulantonio Soderini"): a homophonic alphabet, code signs (che, et, per, io, tu, voi, noi, quando, come and titles), nulls and name numbers 10 to 59. It was identified on 13 November 2026 by matching it to the interlinear decipherments in Ridolfi's own glossed letters LXXIV 107 and 110, and then found to read Alamanni's letter of April as well. Daniel Bourdeau's key-sheet survey had tested 183 only against a different cipher (Mazzinghi 1478), and his sweep of these MAP rows noted that no key sheet had yet been applied to them; we found no earlier application of 183 to these letters.

The letters use more signs than the sheet lists. [key.json](key.json) records each sign's values and where they come from, which is the grade a value carries:

| Provenance | Signs | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| On the sheet | the alphabet and the codes che, et, per; the sheet's nulls | H |
| Contemporary gloss over the sign in a sibling letter | looped-descender 9 = **ss** (glosses "fussi", "messo", LXXIV 107 c.241r); open cup with a descender = **tt** (glosses "tutto", "tratto", LXXIV 110 c.250r); dotted Λ = **non** (gloss "no[n]" before "venga", LXXIV 110 c.250r; the sheet labels this sign "noi") | C |
| Fitted to the text, no gloss seen | closed barred circle = **rr** (arriuando, uorrei; guerra, serrarci); large looped e = **nn** (innanzi, danno, Donnino, Giovanni, hanno); dotted cross = **bb** (habbiate and habbia, one stem; the third use, in *strignerebbe*, is doubtful); closed triangle = **cc** (bocca, faccenda) | S |
| Person and name signs, identity from context | two long-tailed 2s with raised letters, read [il Re] and [Duca o Lodovico]; name number 22 | I |

The doubled-consonant values rest on conventional Italian spellings in letters that elsewhere often write single consonants (*castelo*, *quelo*), so the exact doubling of rr, nn, bb and cc is less secure than the consonant identity.

Two hands, two sets of transcription labels: in Alamanni's letter the open cup (tt) and the crossed-tail 9 (ss) are the same signs as Ridolfi's, and carry Ridolfi's labels `PSI` and `Q` here. Alamanni's d-form and l-form separate on a contact sheet of every d/l sign (`Zd` and `5`); Ridolfi's do not, and his open crossed form is a d/l class (`Z`). The ÷ sign stands for both n and c on the sheet itself.

### Name number 22

The sheet gives 22 as "S. di Pesaro" and Lodovico as 15. Ridolfi's letter LXXIV 107, c. 241r, has the contemporary gloss "S. Lco" written directly over a 22, between the clear words "dove" and "quattro". That establishes 22 = Lodovico for that occurrence; it does not show whether the sheet is a different working list, a local substitution or an error, nor that Alamanni used the same number list. Alamanni's three 22 passages concern Lodovico's intentions, which fits, but those contexts partly assume the expansion. The 22 readings are graded I and rendered "[22: probably S. Lodovico; cf. Ridolfi 107 gloss; sheet: S. di Pesaro]".

### The person signs in Ridolfi

Neither long-tailed 2 is on the sheet or glossed in anything seen (the look-alike in LXXIV 110 c.250r is a plain 2 with raised letters, glossed "B[ernar]do"). The one with a raised a-sign is read [il Re] from four contexts (entering the castle; "sua M[aes]tà" in the next clause; "a[p]resentarsi al"; "a la corte di"). The one with a raised d/l sign is the Duke if the sick man in bed is meant, and Lodovico if the answer to the Pavia citizens is his; the same sign is used in all three places, and the identity is left open.

## How the words are graded

Each cipher word is assigned a tier for how its reading was chosen, then a grade (CONVENTIONS.md section 1):

- **strict**: every sign has one value in key.json and was read without a second shape reading. Grade = the weakest provenance among its signs (H, C or S as in the table above).
- **forced**: a sign is ambiguous (÷ = n/c, the d/l class, signs the reader confused in calibration), but only one choice gives a word. Grade S, or M if a sign is poorly seen. For Ridolfi this was decided against a word list drawn from period Tuscan prose (Machiavelli, *Istorie fiorentine*; Guicciardini, *Storie fiorentine*; Italian Wikisource) and the letters' own clear text; for Alamanni it was decided by inspection. Corpus uniqueness is not historical necessity.
- **lenient**: the choice among the signs' values was made by a language model or by sense. Grade M.
- **doubt**, **unread**: grade M; an unread word gets no reading.
- Person signs and name numbers are grade I whatever their tier, as is any word whose reading supplies the value of an unidentified sign (*a[p]resentarsi*, *mante[?]o*).

`verify.py` enforces the tier rules (a strict word may not contain an ambiguous sign) and checks that every editorial reading spells exactly what the key gives, apart from [bracketed] letters, accents, apostrophes and u/v. Abbreviations are graded at the level of the enciphered letters (*prta* is H; the expansion *pr[efa]ta* is editorial).

The read fractions are coverage, not accuracy. On a blind test (below), about three words in four came out right, so the lenient figures include wrong words.

## Controls and calibration

- **Fit to the glosses.** The sheet's values reproduce Ridolfi's own interlinear decipherments of eight words in LXXIV 107 and 110 (havendo, una volta, questo, stato, salvo, per venire, si, di): 44 of 44 letters, against at most 41% for 2,000 shuffled keys. Those pairs were transcribed with the gloss in view and were how the match was found, so this is a fit check, not a held-out test.
- **Blind calibration, Ridolfi's hand.** Four glossed lines of LXXIV 107 c.241v were transcribed from crops with the gloss masked, then decoded and compared: of 38 glossed words, 28 right, 4 confidently wrong and 6 flagged or unread. After the reader's confusable pairs (s/h, p/l) were widened, the word list recovers two of the four errors; that improvement is measured on the same passage, so it is not a fresh estimate.
- **Calibration, Alamanni's reader.** The same key on LXXIV 107 c.241r lines 6-9, transcribed by the reader of the Alamanni letter: 20 of 24 gloss words agree firmly. The gloss was partly visible while transcribing, so this is not strictly blind.
- **Key-shuffle permutation** (`python3 milan-1494/verify.py --z`, `cipherkit.controls.permutation_z_key`; Italian 4-gram model over the cipherkit `it` corpus with word boundaries): the single-letter values are shuffled among the (sign, chosen value) pairs actually used, and the words read are rescored. Ridolfi z = 18.5, Alamanni z = 13.5 (p = 0.001, the floor for 1,000 shuffles). Because the null keeps the reading's per-occurrence choices, it shows that the key's letter structure is far from random; it does not test individual words. On the working transcriptions with first-choice shapes and first sheet values, the same kind of shuffle gave z = 7.4 and z = 8.0 for Alamanni.
- **Independent review.** The readings were checked against the images and the key by a second model (Codex, 13 November 2026): 70 image spot-checks across the four leaves, an exact-substitution audit of every word, and the provenance of each disputed sign. Its corrections are applied here; the review and the transcribers' reconciliation logs are in [AUDIT.md](AUDIT.md).

## Open points

- Ridolfi 113: two groups across the recto/verso break, between "intenderete" and "da lui a bocca", unread.
- Ridolfi 112: "lo edicto" (line 8) and "strignerebbe" (line 12) doubtful; the person sign RD unresolved between the Duke and Lodovico.
- Alamanni: the run after the first 22 (220v line 11, letters "?-una-starna"); one blotted sign after "l'armata" (221r line 10); three groups after "da poter" (221v line 2, letters "i ear made", where the sense wants something like "poterci far male", which the shapes do not give); the second of the three threats (221v lines 3-4, letters "anresima nela calera o", word division unknown); the clear word after "alla mente del re" (220v line 20).
- Alamanni: *andassi*, *havessi*, *ferla* (or *farla*), *netta*, *Alfonso* (penultimate sign o or u), *mante[?]o* and *[in]dugarlo* are lenient readings, some needing a re-segmentation of the working transcription that the alternatives recorded first did not include.
- The scans are 1272 px wide, the most the ASF server gives; most open places are at the limit of the image, and a new photograph of 220v line 11, 221v lines 2-4 and Ridolfi 113 lines 9 to verso 1 is what would settle them.
- The clear text of both letters was read once and not collated. The historical identifications in the notes (Louis de Luxembourg as Bona's nephew, and so on) have not been checked against reference works.
- The other glossed letters of the same embassy (Niccolini 1492-93, LXXIV docs 6 to 97; Ridolfi 101-110, 114) were not used beyond calibration; a full exemplar table built from them would test the fitted values.

## Checking against the images

Every image used is listed in [images.tsv](images.tsv): ASF image id, filza, document, leaf, what it was used for, our copy's pixel size and sha256, and the viewer URL. It covers the four leaves of Alamanni's letter, all leaves of Ridolfi 112, 113 and 115 (including the versos and address leaves that carry no cipher), the glossed siblings 107 and 110 used for the key fit, the blind calibration, the "S. Lco" gloss over 22 (107 c. 241r) and the "no[n]" gloss (110 c. 250r), and both sides of the key sheet LXXII 183.

To download them all: `python3 milan-1494/fetch_images.py` (standard library; writes to `milan-1494/images/`, which is gitignored, and compares each file with our copy's hash). The script documents the route: the viewer page names a pyramid TIFF, and the JPEG comes from the archive's IIPImage server at the 1272 px maximum width. As of 13 November 2026 the server answers only from Italian IP addresses; plain GET requests from an Italian VPN exit work, with no login.

Citations: transcription rows are keyed `leaf:line.n` (for example `220v:6.1`). Leaf names use the folio of the image (`220v` is c. 220 v, image 81734; `112r` is c. 256 r, image 81807; `113r`/`113v` are c. 257 r/v; `115r` is c. 263 r). Line numbers count written lines from the top of the leaf, except Ridolfi `113r` and `113v`, which number the cipher lines only (113r:1 is the first cipher line in the lower third of c. 257 r). The Codex review in `audit/codex-review.md` gives pixel coordinates for the crops it checked.

## Failure log

- 13 November 2026: the ASF image server timed out from the US, the Netherlands and about forty other test locations, and answered only from Italy; reached through an Italian VPN exit.
- A first working transcription merged three distinct signs (flagged o = e, underlined o = o, flat-topped σ = p) and did not recognise the doubled-consonant signs, which left many groups unread. Fresh transcriptions made without sight of it, then reconciled with it sign by sign, give the present readings (AUDIT.md).
- Language-model choices were used to rank alternatives, never to settle them: wherever a model or the sense chose, the word is lenient and grade M.

## Files

- `key.json`: every sign label, its values, what the sheet itself gives, provenance grade, shape and basis.
- `ridolfi-transcription.tsv`, `alamanni-transcription.tsv`: one row per unit in page order, with the chosen value of every ambiguous sign, other shape readings in braces, poorly seen signs marked, tier, editorial reading and note.
- `verify.py`: reproduces `reading.txt` and the counts, enforces the rules above, exits non-zero if anything is stale; `--z` runs the key-shuffle control.
- `reading.txt`: generated word list (key output, editorial reading, tier, grade).
- `ridolfi-reading.md`, `alamanni-reading.md`: the letters in Italian and English.
- `images.tsv`, `fetch_images.py`: every ASF image used, with hashes of our copies, and a downloader (see "Checking against the images").
- `AUDIT.md` and `audit/`: how the readings were made and checked, the independent review, and the reconciliation logs.
