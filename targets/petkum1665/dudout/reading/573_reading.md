## English translation of the enciphered passages

Now that the French ambassadors are leaving, they are beginning to speak more softly [...] so that nobody notices it [...] the business [...] had no fear of anyone [...] those who have served their apprenticeship laugh at such tricks. I [hold for certain] the rupture between France and England, and that so far England has no friend but the Bishop of Münster. [You see then] what reason they have here to act the knowing one [...] the King of England within the kingdom [...] if affairs went badly, I do not know whether a defeat would not be enough to put the malcontents under arms, [above all] the Scots, who are very French. [...] the ‹399› ‹40› recall of the ambassadors of the Most Christian King [might delay it further, if it] does not break it off entirely, for I imagine that His Majesty will want to communicate his steps to the Most Christian King, whose ambassadors leave fully persuaded that His Majesty is a most affectionate friend of their master, but that he needs a sum of French money. [Meanwhile I have not failed to] persuade the Spanish ambassador of the same, [who shows much inclination] to some close alliance between His Majesty, the Emperor and the King of Spain, [which might deserve the consideration of] His Majesty if England were of the party; [but what] can we do without naval forces, and [even so] France will be the stronger [...] at the court of Stockholm they persuade M. Juel that they are very English there, to please the minister of England in this way. [The ablest man in the world] can be deceived [...] the English, that His Majesty believes it so ‹338› ‹40› and that he truly means to join them under the conditional treaty. [...] of Bergen [and then on the] conduct of His Majesty in this ‹370› here, [which was highly] praised [as] wise and generous [...] that conduct [is plain to] the eyes of all.
