Charles V to Ferrante Gonzaga: do not touch Parma

→

Go to the full text ↓

Four days after Paul III died, and before he could know it, the Emperor told his governor of Milan in cipher not to make terms with Ottavio Farnese over Parma, and when he might still seize the city.

Daniel Bourdeau · posted 3 November 2026 · updated 14 November 2026

The letters
Charles V to Ferrante Gonzaga, governor of Milan, signed Carlos and countersigned Vargas. The main one is dated Brussels, 14 November 1549, with about 110 lines of signs. Four shorter passages come from 13 May, 30 September, 20 November and 23 December 1549. Since 14 November 2026 the entry also holds Prince Philip’s letters to Gonzaga in the same cipher (1546–1553).
Where
Biblioteca Estense Universitaria, Modena, Autografoteca Campori, Lettera K, Karl V, sfasc. IV cc. 60-63 and siblings; Lettera F, Felipe II, sfasc. I and II. Not on DECODE, by a search of the DECODE list on the names (Estense catalogue entries 1560 and 1561).
The cipher
The Imperial Cipher of 1544–1554: the shape of a sign gives the consonant and a mark gives the vowel. Clear words stand in the text as code words.
How it was deciphered
Twice and, by the two records, apart. The project used the key as reconstructed by Bertomeu Masiá and pictured by Tomokiyo, extended by hand. WAINUT rebuilt a table from the decipherments of the time on Prince Philip’s letters, froze it, and read the same letter with it. The 14 November letter has no decipherment of its own.
How far
Sure words only: 837 of 1,007 tokens (83%) in the project’s reading, 817 of 910 words (90%) in WAINUT’s. The two transcriptions give the same value at 2,312 of 2,523 aligned signs (92%).
What it says
Making terms with Ottavio would bring little and could mean war with the Church. “Nunca havemos querido emprender” anything there by way of war. Gonzaga may act only in two named cases, and then with dissimulation.
Still open
23 places where the two readings differ on a sure word; three code words; the stretches of the project’s reading that do not form words; three of Philip’s letters. No person has checked a passage against the image.

01 The letters

The Estense holds 151 letters from Charles V to Ferrante Gonzaga, most in clear. Nine were flagged as partly in cipher. Five use the sign cipher treated here, and each of those carries its cipher as passages inside a clear letter, except that the 14 November letter is almost solid cipher from the middle of c. 60r to the top of c. 62r. It ends “Dat. en Bruselas a xiiij de Noviembre M.D.xlviiij”, with the signatures of Carlos and Vargas on c. 62v.

The numeric letters of 15 July 1549 and 11 August 1542 and the letter of 1 July 1552 use other ciphers, so they are not part of this page. The ninth, cc. 131–132, is a letter of the ambassador in France in another key; it has its own page, as the letter of 1542 has its own.

The same letters, and Prince Philip’s letters to Gonzaga in the same cipher, were worked on by WAINUT, a Dutch AI company, on the same two days (2 and 3 November 2026) and, by the two records, apart. Brice Dudout pointed out that the two pieces of work concern the same Campori letters (issues #615 and #618), and since 14 November 2026 they are one entry: one key, two readings. Sections 02 to 04 give the project’s reading; section 05 gives WAINUT’s and the comparison.

02 What the Emperor says

Ottavio Farnese had been trying to recover Parma from his grandfather the Pope, and Gonzaga had reported a chance to make terms with him. Charles answers in cipher. The whole text is under Full texts below; these are the parts that run, in the project’s reading. The code words are left as written: Pollo is the Pope and nerp France.

Ottavio is a poor foundation. He has no money, being “en desgracia del Pollo”, and nowhere to draw troops from except the lands of the Church. To carry the matter forward Gonzaga would have to declare himself, which would be “romper la guerra contra la yglesia”, since “Su Santidad” holds Parma in the Church’s name, not the Farnese house’s.

The policy. “En Parma y en Plazencia nunca havemos querido emprender hasta qui ninguna cosa por via de guerra avierta.” If terms were made and the Pollo died, “se vernia luego a las manos”, and Charles would find himself “embaraçado y cargado por ambas partes”.

The fear. Terms might give the Pollo an excuse “passar adelante mas desvergonçadamente en dar Parma a Horacio”, making “una fro(?)ntera” against the Emperor (one sign of the word is doubtful).

The order. “No deveys en ninguna manera entrar por el presente en capitulacion con el dicho Ottavio.” Two exceptions follow. If Camillo Orsini tries a stroke “en sede vacante o en vida del Pollo”, Gonzaga may do what seems best, provided it is justified and has a safe way out. If Orazio or someone else enters Parma, for instance by an agreement between the Pollo and “el rey de nerp”, Gonzaga is to find a way to make himself master of the city, “con la desteridad y dissymulacion” that Charles trusts him to use. Otherwise, while “Parma [is] en manos del Pollo”, nothing is to be undertaken against it.

The Pollo is the Pope. The letter never says so. But c. 60v has “teniendo Su Santidad como agora tiene a Parma” where c. 62r has “estando Parma en manos del Pollo”, and c. 61v speaks of “sede vacante o en vida del Pollo”. Paul III died on 10 November 1549, and the letter of 14 November was clearly written before the news came. By 20 November the Emperor writes in clear of “este tiempo de Sede vacante”. A list of this cipher’s code words drawn up at Simancas in 1866 from ciphered letters of 1545 in Estado, Flandes, leg. 501 (Archivo General de Simancas, D/00203 no. 243; pointed out by Brice Dudout, issue #618, after Satoshi Tomokiyo) has pollo for Papa, and a letter of 1552 that Tomokiyo assigns to the same cipher is deciphered that way in its margin (no sign of that letter is transcribed here).

03 The other passages

  • 20 November 1549 (cc. 64r–64v): five short passages, written after the news. “En ninguna manera se mueva ninguna cosa en Parma”, no war on the Church, and no attack on the Farnese state at such a time. The clerk’s margin notes give the same sense, and they were in view. The last two lines of c. 64r are not deciphered.
  • 30 September 1549 (c. 50r): someone goes “con fin de tractar al prior de lombardia”, castellan of the castle, so as to throw him out and put in Mons. de Brissac. A clear margin note summarises it, and it was also in view.
  • 13 May 1549 (c. 30r): where to find money and materials for a work, with no show of the intention. Slip c. 29 is the decipherment of the time.
  • 23 December 1549 (c. 76r): on Nice and the Prior of Lombardy, sent to live in the castle. c. 77 is the decipherment of the time.

04 Method

The slip on c. 29 gave the first values. Tomokiyo’s Cryptiana then identified the system and pictures Bertomeu Masiá’s reconstruction of the key, which shows the rule: base shape for the consonant, mark for the vowel. The key image is small and does not cover every form, so the sign table was extended from the letters themselves.

The cipher was cropped into numbered panels at about seven times size, and each sign was named and given a value in a sign file, line by line. The main session did c. 60r block 1 and c. 60v. Subagents with the same brief did c. 60r block 2, c. 61r, c. 61v, c. 62r and the short passages. In all there are 3,032 signs: 2,780 with a value, 128 doubtful, 51 taken as nulls, 25 clear words and 48 unread.

The text is continuous across the pages, which is one check on it: c. 60r ends “romper la guerra contra la yglesia o” and c. 60v goes on “no seria … que romper la guerra con[tra] la yglesia” (the project’s sign file has an unread sign after “con”, settled as “contra” in section 05; WAINUT takes the last line of c. 60r as a repeat struck by the clerk); c. 60v ends “lo qual no con” and c. 61r begins “vergia”.

The statistical test (docs/_validate.py, order-4 model of Golden Age Spanish) passes. The reading scores −1.96 per letter, against −3.99 for random keys (9.5 sd) and −2.56 for keys annealed onto shuffled signs (45.5 sd), and an annealer on the real text reaches only −2.34.

05 A second, independent reading, and the comparison

WAINUT’s work. WAINUT transcribed twelve letters of Charles V and Prince Philip to Gonzaga from the Estense images. Its table was not taken from a printed key: it was built by setting Philip’s letter of 8 March 1549 (c. 12r) against the decipherment of the time on c. 14r, extended on the letter of 23 May 1547 against c. 11, and then frozen. With that table, extended on the letter itself, WAINUT read Charles’s letter of 14 November 1549. That the table was frozen before the Charles letters were opened is by WAINUT’s own record; that the two readings were made apart is by the two records (the files and notes of each side), not stamped from outside; neither folder cited the other before 14 November 2026.

Where the project’s reading has sign values that do not form words, WAINUT’s mostly has words, some marked as repairs or proposals: “sostener y aprobar su derecho por mejor que el imperio”, “enflaquecer el derecho”, “es de creer que por pura imposibilidad”, “al cabo con su aguelo”, “con quien esta hecha la tregua”. Where WAINUT has a proposal or a hole, the project is sure at 25 places (“quedava”, “voluntad”, “sacar la gente”, “como desesperado”). These are the lines to reread on the image with both tables.

Code words. Values now carried into both readings: Pollo Papa, mus para, inta Su Santidad, em Franceses, gien vuestra, saa Duque, and nerp (WAINUT reads nep) Francia. mus, em and gien were already in WAINUT’s reading from its frozen table and entered the project’s from that table and the Simancas list; inta was corrected in both from the list (both had taken it for the Church); saa fills a hole in WAINUT’s. Six code words of WAINUT’s frozen table, learned from the decipherments of the time, have the same value on the Simancas list of 1866. So Orazio would be put into Parma “in the name of the French”, and Parma is to stay in the hands “of His Holiness”.

The count. One rule for both: a word counts only if it is sure. In the project’s reading a doubtful or unread sign, an open code word, or a run of sign values that forms no word makes a hole: 837 of 1,007 tokens (83%; 129 of the tokens are such sign values, so the share of words is somewhat higher). In WAINUT’s reading a repair, a proposal and a hole do not count: 817 of 910 words (90%). WAINUT’s own looser figure, with its marked repairs, is 864 of 910 (94.9%). The 93% this page carried before was the share of signs given a value, not of words. Neither reading reaches 95%, so the extent stays partial.

Prince Philip’s letters (Lettera F, Felipe II; all WAINUT’s work). Three passages are deciphered in part: 4 August 1548, c. 10r, on Genoa and Henry II’s descent into Italy (75 of 80 words sure); 24 November 1551, c. 28r, on Nice and Siena (56 of 66); 24 November 1551, c. 26r (75 of 91; a passage on the vicariate is unsafe). The letters of 8 March 1549 and 23 May 1547 have their decipherments of the time, and the table was built on them. Three more, of 25 June 1546, 14 March 1553 and one catalogued under 16 June 1563, are decoded from a rough pass and give no sense yet. WAINUT also found that the letters of 30 September and 20 November 1549 carry a decipherment in the margin beside every cipher passage (seven and six by its reading file and the project’s notes; for 30 September WAINUT’s own files disagree, its status table and its file of the margins giving six), and that the letter catalogued 1563 opens “El Principe” and is addressed to Ferrante, who died in 1557, so it is earlier than 1556. Two further letters of Philip in this cipher, pointed out by Brice Dudout, are not transcribed.

06 What remains uncertain

  • Not a controlled test. Every value was chosen with the sentence in view, knowing what would make Spanish. The statistical test speaks for the key as a whole, not for any one word. Only one line of the subagents’ work (c. 61v line 15) was checked against its crop, and no person has checked any. The comparison with the second reading is the first cross-check either has had, and it was made on the files, not on the image.
  • Code words. vela or bela, a place news comes from, is not on the Simancas list of 1866 (Rome is WAINUT’s proposal). et ud (Embajador de Inglaterra on the list) and tu (lea) give no sense where they stand. France is nec with an end mark on the list, nerp in one reading and nep in the other: the sense is settled, the letters of the code word are not.
  • The 23 open differences between the two readings, listed in section 05. Each is one sign on an image that is open to anyone.
  • Stretches that do not form sentences. c. 60v lines 7–8 and 23–27; c. 61r lines 4–5 and 16–18; c. 61v line 18 (“la pe gu l ro n”, where the second reading has “la tregua”). This is the project’s reading; the second reading has words at most of these places.
  • Doubtful spellings left as the signs give them. “Hallarse” after “verniamos”; “arrordio” for acordio; “su rr esso” for suceso. Gamma is rr in the key but wants c here. “Plazencia” rests on an N-shaped sign taken as p from the words it occurs in.
  • Signs with dots below. Most drop out inside a word (“car.gado”, “quisie.sse”), but some carry their plain value (c. 60r line 12), and the end of c. 64r gives no Spanish either way.
  • People. The signs give Ursino and Horacio. Taking them as Camillo Orsini and Orazio Farnese is from general knowledge. “Dragut” for “Drigui” on c. 62r is a guess.
  • Prior work. Bertomeu Masiá’s La guerra secreta de Carlos V contra el Papa (2009) studies this very affair and its ciphers, in Granvelle’s correspondence. It could not be read online, so these letters may already be in print there. A web search found no printed text of the 14 November letter, but the search was not exhaustive.

# Charles V to Ferrante Gonzaga, 30 Sep and 20 Nov 1549: transcription jobs

Both letters carry a contemporary decipherment in the margin beside every cipher passage. They are not breaks. The text below is the marginal
decipherment, in my reading, with abbreviations expanded (`clear_margins_charles.txt`). The cipher is in `transcription_ch1549_0930_1120_blind.txt`, one blind
pass, and its decoding in `decryption_ch1549_0930_1120_blind.txt`. That decoding is unrepaired and has errors. It is not yet a line-by-line edition.

## 30 Sep 1549, Brussels, cc. 50-1r to 50-3r (leaves numbered 50-01, 50-02, 51)

Spanish, from the margins:

1. y aunque yua con fin de tractar al Prior de Lombardia, castellano que es del castillo, de tal manera que fuesse tanto como echarle de ally por meter dentro a Mos. de Brissiu
2. Veays que lo del tractado de aquella ciudad
3. Una carta que el Conde de Tenda screuia al Condestable, la qual se hallo en poder de vn Frances que Ingleses mataron en vna escaramuza junto a Bolonia, que ellos mesmos nos la han traydo, y
4. que se tenia sperança del dicho tractado de Niça
5. y buena guarda de aquel castillo y lo demas lo que vieredes ser menester y conuenir para obuiar a los inconuenientes
6. se ha entendido que las personas de las que les se tenia sospecha que interuiniessen en el dicho tractado
7. ciertos auisos que han venido de Francia cerca del frayle que teneys preso, porque scriue nuestro Embaxador que seruiran mucho para effecto de su deposicion

English:

1. and although it was aimed at dealing with the Prior of Lombardy, who is castellan of the castle, in such a way that it would amount to throwing him out of there, in order to put Mons. de Brissac inside
2. See that the matter of the plot on that city
3. A letter that the Count of Tende was writing to the Constable, which was found on a Frenchman whom the English killed in a skirmish near Boulogne, and which they themselves have brought to us, and
4. that hope was held of the said plot on Nice
5. and good guard of that castle, and for the rest what you see to be necessary and fitting to prevent the troubles
6. it has been learned that the persons who were suspected of taking part in the said plot
7. certain advices that have come from France about the friar you hold prisoner, since our Ambassador writes that they will be of great use for his deposition

The cipher differs from the margin at passage 2. The signs at K-06 and K-07 give `lo del tractado de aquel [ca]stillo`. The margin says "aquella ciudad". The first
sign of K-07 was checked on a zoomed crop. The signs were read by a reader with no table and no clear text, so this difference is not steered.

Contents: a French plot on Nice, the Duke of Savoy's stronghold, aimed at getting the castellan out and Brissac in. An intercepted letter from the Count of Tende to
Montmorency came to Charles by way of the English at Boulogne. An Italian copy headed "Transcritto de la lettera del Conte di Tenda al Conestabile" follows on cc. 51-52.
Its date looks like August 1549 at Marseille; I saw it at contact-sheet scale only.

Context: Boulogne was held by the English and besieged by France in 1549. Nice mattered because Charles needed Savoy's coast between Genoa and Provence. Ferrante had
to watch it from Milan.

What the clear text hides: the clear text speaks of the Duke of Savoy's wish to go to Nice and of the need for vigilance. The method of the plot, the name Brissac, the
intercepted letter and its route, and the evidence against the imprisoned friar are in cipher only.

## 20 Nov 1549, Brussels, cc. 64r-65r

Spanish, from the margins:

1. a Parma en nombre de la yglesia
2. mueua ninguna cosa en Parma de nuestra parte ni en otro lugar
3. que no queremos mouer guerra a la yglesia
4. offender y occupar en esta occasion el estado de la casa Farnesa por la vezindad que tiene con el del Duque de Florencia y
5. tentar en tal tiempo
6. pareciere otra cosa y dieren occasion se podra facilmente hazer

English:

1. to Parma in the name of the Church
2. let nothing be moved in Parma on our part, nor in any other place
3. that we do not wish to make war on the Church
4. to attack and occupy on this occasion the state of the House of Farnese, because of its neighbourhood with that of the Duke of Florence, and
5. to try at such a time
6. if something else should seem fit and they should give occasion, it can easily be done

Contents and context: this is the reply to the news of the death of Paul III (10 Nov 1549). The clear text deals with the conclave, the election of a pope, and Don Diego
de Mendoza at Rome. The cipher repeats the order of 14 Nov in a new situation: no move on Parma or elsewhere, no war on the Church, while conceding that it could easily be done
later if occasion arose. Passage 4 follows a clear phrase on the opportunity the Duke of Florence might have. I saw that phrase at contact-sheet scale only, so who proposed the attack on the
Farnese state is not shown here.

What the clear text hides: the clear text is about the sede vacante and a free election. The refusal to move, and the option kept open for later, are in cipher only.
