Radio messages of the Flak Command Black Forest, 17 May 1940

Go to the full text ↓

Two Luftwaffe radio messages in a double columnar transposition were read after the day’s keys were found from the recipient’s fair copy; 1,016 of 1,023 letters are right as plaintext. A third message, of 4 June 1940, was added on 12 November 2026 with the key of that day, which, by his own record, Brice Dudout recovered the same day as Harumi-Matsuba (who published it first). On 14 November 2026 Jacob Houtrouw added the keys of nineteen more days, 9 April to 1 June 1940 (13,215 letters).

The messages
Two radio messages of 17 May 1940 of the Flugabwehrkommando Schwarzwald: frame 0993 in four parts (Luftgaukommando VII to the command) and frames 1025–1027 in two parts (the command itself), 1,023 letters of text.
Where
National Archives, Record Group 242, microfilm T405 roll 9 (NAID 332033680); catalogue 704.
The cipher
Double columnar transposition with a 24-column and a 28-column key; the first group of each part is an indicator.
How it was read
Harumi-Matsuba, with an AI agent, fitted the plaintext of parts 3 and 4 of frame 0993 to the recipient’s fair copy and ran known-plaintext searches for the keys from part 4 (other AI agents served as image judges and second reader); the project decrypted all six parts with its own code and got his letter strings (with part 3 put in order, as he describes), against a random-key trigram control (66 against a best of 19). The images were not seen here.
Added 12 Nov 2026
The five-part radio message of 4 June 1940 (frames 0863–0867, 928 letters of text) with its own keys of 19 and 22 columns: Harumi-Matsuba published them first (11 Nov 2026); Brice Dudout, working with AI agents, found, by his record, the same keys the same day from his own transcription and tested them once on part 4, held out. 124 of its 129 words read as certain, 5 are marked probable on doubtful letters.
Added 14 Nov 2026
The keys of nineteen more days, 9 April to 1 June 1940 (21 messages in 78 parts, 13,215 letters of text, 8 more lost from the film), found by Jacob Houtrouw, working with AI models, by known-plaintext searches with the clear copies on the reel as cribs (issue #602). The project’s own decoder gives his plain texts for all nineteen days; 79 letters do not make their words as transcribed.
What they say
Transfer of Flak platoons of Reserve Light Flak Battalion 932 and an anti-aircraft machine-gun company to the command and a report of one platoon in position west of Tüllingen; on 4 June 1940, seven Flak units moved into position at stations and towns of the Black Forest to protect army unloadings. The nineteen days added on 14 November are reports of Flak units changing position with the hour from which each was ready to fire, an order of 9 April to raise readiness, and a list of new reference points of 23 May.

01 The messages

Two radio messages of 17 May 1940 from the file of the Luftgaukommando VII (Munich) and its Flak units: National Archives, Record Group 242, microfilm T405 roll 9 (NAID 332033680). The first, frame 0993, is a typed teleprinter copy sent to the Flugabwehrkommando Schwarzwald in four parts; the recipient’s hand-written fair copy is on frames 0994–0995. The second, frames 1025–1027, is a hand-written radio form of the Flugabwehrkommando Schwarzwald itself, in two parts; its teleprinter original is on frame 1024. Both are in five-letter groups. The ledger (catalogue 704) listed the whole reel, about 70 such forms (the catalogue’s estimate), as a test bed for a double transposition. Harumi-Matsuba read these two with Anthropic Claude and sent the transcriptions, the keys and the reading; the project could not reach the scans and checked the keys against his transcriptions.

On 12 November 2026 a third message was added: the radio message of 4 June 1940 in five parts, frames 0863–0867 (179, 190, 194, 186 and 204 letters with the indicator groups, 928 letters of text; parts 1–4 in ink, part 5 in pencil in another hand). It follows frame 0862, a typed teleprinter message of the Flugabwehrkommando Schwarzwald Ic of 4 June 1940, 11.45, whose text it carries. By his record, Brice Dudout transcribed all five parts from the NARA images on 28 October 2026, before any key was known, with an AI agent, and corrected six doubtful letters on the images on 12 November.

02 The cipher and the keys

A double columnar transposition. The plaintext is written in rows in a grid 24 columns wide and read off by columns in the order of a numeric key; the result is written in a grid 28 columns wide and read off the same way. The first group of each part is an indicator and not text. The keys for 17 May 1940:

The contributor fixed the plaintext of parts 3 and 4 of frame 0993 from the recipient’s fair copy, by fitting spellings to the letter counts, and found the keys with known-plaintext searches of his own from part 4. The other parts and the whole of the second message were then decrypted with the same two keys, but the transcriptions were not left untouched: part 3 decrypts only with its lines 2 and 3 put back in order and letter 113 read T, and the contributor corrected some letters of the hand-written transcription (and three Q/O letters of part 4) against the images after decrypting, so the transcriptions are partly informed by the decryption. The same system, with other keys, is on the 1939 exercise message and key card of frames 349–350, which he used to test his tools.

Checked here, with an own implementation written from the description: the two keys undo the six parts of his transcriptions to the six strings of his plaintext.txt, letter for letter (1,023 letters). A count of frequent German trigrams gives 66 for these decryptions against a mean of 8.9 and a best of 19 for 300 random pairs of keys of the same lengths. That shows the keys make German, not how many letters are right.

The message of 4 June 1940 has other keys, of 19 and 22 columns (the plaintext written in rows of 19, then the result in rows of 22):

Harumi-Matsuba put these keys in his repository on 11 November 2026 (08:28 UTC) and in a comment on issue #364 (10:41 UTC). Brice Dudout’s record is that he reached the same keys about fourteen hours later and found the comment only on 12 November, while preparing his pull request; the project has only that record (a single commit, self-declared times). His search used the plaintext of parts 1 and 2 taken from frame 0862 and fitted to the letter counts, found the 22-column key by annealing over all pairs of lengths (19, 22 stood out alone) with the idea of the public kpb.c, then the 19-column key on German quadgrams. The keys were then frozen with their scorer and hashed, and one measurement was made on part 4, held out: German quadgrams −4.993 per letter against −6.643 for the best of 400 controls, and agreement with the plaintext predicted from frame 0862 of 0.989 against 0.227. It passed on all three conditions set in advance. Two limits: the predicted plaintext is the text of the teleprinter copy, so the agreement shows the key is right and does not read new text; and before the protocol was frozen the raw NARA OCR of about 110 characters of part 4 was printed once, not analysed. The project decrypted all five parts with its own code, written from the key, and got the contributor’s strings letter for letter (928 letters); on frame 0863 (part 1) all 179 letters of the transcription were compared with the NARA image and agree, with the u/n hook of the hand as the one open shape. The other four parts were not compared with the images. The transcription differs from Harumi-Matsuba’s in 7 of 953 letters.

03 Nineteen more days

On 14 November 2026 Jacob Houtrouw (who writes as Jake Tschakka) posted, in issue #602, the keys of nineteen more days of the reel, each with the transcription of the cipher sheets, the plain text, a reading and an English translation: 21 messages in 78 parts. AI models working at his direction read the frame images, made the cribs, wrote and ran the search and wrote the readings; he set the tests and verified. Every message has a clear copy on a neighbouring frame, and that copy, turned into the radio spelling, was the crib: the second key was searched first, as in Harumi-Matsuba’s method, then the first. By his record, each run followed a plan that was written and hashed before it; the project has the hashes and times he gives and no timestamp file.

Checked here: a decoder written for the project from the keys and transcriptions in the issue gives his plain text on all nineteen days, 13,215 letters, with 8 letters lost from the film (sheet backs that were not filmed, on 21, 23 and 24 May) and 79 letters that do not make their words as transcribed, at the places he lists. On thirty frequent German trigrams the real keys score between 44 and 321 a day, against 10 to 41 for the best of 300 shuffled pairs of keys. Two cipher frames were compared with the NARA images: frame 1114 (2 May, 98 letters) agrees in full, and frame 0931 (23 May, 90 letters) in all but one letter, which he changed from i to c after the key was known and which looks like i on the sheet. Frame 1112, the clear copy of 2 May, carries the text of his reading. The other 82 cipher frames and the other clear copies were not compared, and the search was not repeated.

The indicator rule is not the same on every day: on 6, 10 and 11 May only the first part begins with an indicator group, and on 9 May one indicator is written with six letters. Four pairs of days have keys of the same two lengths (9 April and 8 May, 6 and 29 May, 10 and 15 May, 11 and 21 May), with different keys.

He reports his failures with the rest: 3 May (frames 1100–1105), the second message of 9 April (frames 1120–1121) and 28 May (frames 905–906) gave no key in two sealed attempts each, and on 11, 12 and 29 May and for part 2 of 6 May a first attempt missed before a changed crib or parse found the key. Six of the nineteen messages are ones Brice Dudout had tried earlier without a key.

That is the contributor’s division of the letter strings into words, with his spelling conventions (X between words and names, Q for CH, digits spelled out, KLAMMER for a bracket). In English: Flak Group Friedrichshafen hands over to the Flak Command Black Forest the 1st and 3rd platoons of the 3rd battery of Reserve Light Flak Battalion 932 (Herbertingen), to be moved by the command and employed as agreed by telephone, and places the 3rd platoon of Anti-aircraft Machine-gun Reserve Company 304 (Sigmaringen) at the protected object Herbertingen; new positions and readiness to fire are to be reported. In the second message the 1st platoon of 2./932 (2 cm) was assigned to the command on 16 May, has been ready to fire since 22.30 hours in position 3031 west of Tüllingen, and is subordinated to Battalion 942. Both messages exist in a contemporary form beside the cipher (the fair copy and the teleprinter original), so no unknown text was read here; what is new is the key of the day.

In English: to protect army unloadings, seven Flak units of the Flak Command Black Forest moved into position on 3 June 1940 (the last on 4 June) from stations and towns of the plain to Sankt Georgen, Triberg, Peterzell, Haslach, Marbach, Hornberg and Villingen, and reported ready to fire between 19.45 and 21.00 hours (the last at 04.35). The radio text differs from the teleprinter copy on frame 0862 only in spelling and in a file number. Read as sentences it has 129 words: 124 certain and 5 probable (SEIT in part 4; NAQ, JUNI, NR and STRIQ in part 5), each probable one resting on a doubtful letter (g or q, m or n, c or e), none restored or unresolved. By the README rule, which counts guessed words as holes, 124 of 129 words (96.1%) read as sense. Contributor’s own count: 129 of 129 words read as sense.

The nineteen days added on 14 November 2026 are of the same kind. Most are reports of the command’s section Ic that a battery or platoon changed position and since what hour it has been ready to fire, for example, on 2 May: 4./R 321 (Eisb.) nach Stellungswechsel in neuer Stellung Bad Krozingen seit 2.5.1940 05.00 Uhr feuerbereit (after a change of position, the railway battery 4./R 321 has been ready to fire in its new position at Bad Krozingen since 05.00 on 2 May 1940). The message of 9 April is an order of Luftgaukommando VII to raise readiness: a practice firing at Rust-Oggau is broken off and a sound-locator course cancelled. The second message of 23 May adds three reference points to the numbering of Flak Group Black Forest South (Mönchenstein, Zeiningen, Laufenburg). The full radio texts, the German and the English are under Full texts, day by day, in the contributor’s words.

05 What remains uncertain

  • Seven of the 1,023 letters are wrong as plaintext (about 0.7%): five in the typed text of frame 0993 (BATBERIE, TRANSPORTIWRT, FERNNUENDLIQER, and an X and a Q in part 4), which the contributor takes for errors of the sender or the relay, and two in the hand-written message (VIEENULL, GHDOS), where the handwriting cannot settle e or R, h or k.
  • The transcriptions were made by an AI from the NARA frames; the contributor’s blind second reading by another model agrees on 99.2% of frame 0993 and 93.8% to 95.4% of frames 1025–1027 (his figures). The 17 May frames have not been compared with the images. Of the 4 June message only frame 0863 (part 1, all 179 letters) was compared with the NARA image, by the integration check; parts 2–5 were not.
  • The five probable words of the 4 June message rest on letters the images leave open (g and q, m and n are not distinct in these hands); the letter c for e in SEIT (part 4, group 5) was not re-read on the image. Six of the 928 letters are wrong as plaintext (five doubtful, one slip of the sender). The key of 4 June was already public when the contributor’s test ran, and the order of events rests on his own record. His attempt on six other messages of the roll (frames 889–894, 978–985, 1039–1040, 1049–1050, 1056–1057, 1113–1114) found no key: the cribs taken from the NARA OCR of the teleprinter copies were too inexact.
  • In the nineteen days added on 14 November 2026, 79 of 13,215 letters do not make their words as transcribed and 8 are lost from the film; the contributor judged the 79 to be slips of the sheets, from the images, after the key was known. The transcriptions were made by an AI and corrected after the key was known; no human has compared them with the images, and the project compared two cipher frames of 84. The sealed plans, the search and the agreement with the clear copies are by the contributor’s record.
  • How the key follows from the indicator group is not known; the 1939 key card lists four three-letter indicator groups but no rule for this day.
  • 3 May, the second message of 9 April and 28 May were tried by Jacob Houtrouw without a key; they and the other unread days of the reel have other keys, and catalogue 704 stays open for them.
  • No statistical validation by the project’s n-gram test, which is made for substitutions; the trigram control above stands in for it.

# 1 June 1940: English translation, by Jacob Houtrouw (issue #602), copied from the issue as posted on 14 Nov 2026

#### The message (teleprinter copy: frame 0888, SRO 05, accepted 1 June 1940 13.15, passed on 14.30 and 14.50)

Secret command matter. To protect army unloadings, the following moved into position: I/2./942 from the Schluchsee dam to the Donaueschingen road bridge, ready to fire since 22.49 on 31 May 1940; II/2./942 from Schwarzabruck to Immendingen station, ready since 22.49 on 31 May; I/Light Reserve Flak Battery 453 from Forbach to Horb station, ready since 22.20 on 31 May; 4./321 (Eis) from Offenburg to the Hüfingen sawmill, ready since 03.20 on 1 June; II/4./251 from Oberkirch to the viaduct on the Hattingen–Tuttlingen railway, ready since 10.00 on 1 June. Flak Command Black Forest, I c, 212/40, secret command matter.
