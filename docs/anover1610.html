Philip III to the Count of Añover, Lerma, 20 May 1610

Go to the full text ↓

A royal order of ten ciphered lines with no decipherment bound in, deciphered in part with a key rebuilt from the glossed letters beside it: Dutchmen have set up a factory at Pinda on the coast of Angola, and the count is to see it remedied.

The letter
A real cédula of Philip III to Rodrigo Niño Laso, Count of Añover, dated at Lerma on 20 May 1610. The body is ten lines of cipher on f. 53r, with no decipherment on or with the leaf.
Where
Madrid, Biblioteca Histórica Municipal, I 242,29 (Memoria de Madrid 424541; Hispana idBib 58678726). Catalogue entry 1988 of this project; not a DECODE record that the project knows of.
The cipher
A mixed nomenclator: numbers and letter-syllable signs that stand for letters, syllables and whole words, several signs for the same value, groups of unequal length. No key sheet of the series is known.
How it was deciphered
Alberto Garvía García matched the cipher of four sibling letters with their clear texts of the time, which gave 66 group values, and applied them to this letter. The project re-derived the reading from his transcription and table and counted the share strictly.
What it says
Word has come that Dutchmen have gone to set up a factory at Pinda on the coast of Angola, against the truce; the count is to see it remedied without sending anyone to “the Islands”, and to report.
Still open
Twelve of the 89 words are not sure: four codes and two signs have a value from the context or a single parallel, two looped signs are taken as nulls, and two signs are in question. The project has not compared the transcription with the manuscript.
Credit
Transcription, key and reading by Alberto Garvía García, with AI assistance and without an independent palaeographic review, as he states. Brice Dudout sent an independent transcription of the leaf and his alignment of a further glossed letter of the family.

01 The letter

The Biblioteca Histórica Municipal de Madrid holds a series of royal orders in cipher from Philip III to Rodrigo Niño Laso, Count of Añover, of the Council of War, of 1610 and 1611. Eight of them have the decipherment of the time bound in on a slip. Four leaves have none, and I 242,29 is one of them: that is why the series entered this project’s catalogue as entry 1988. The digitised item has five pages; the cipher is on the second, f. 53r, and closes with the date in clear, “de Lerma a 20 de Mayo de 1610”. The reverse carries an archival note about Angola.

The page shows no image of the leaf. The images are on Memoria de Madrid, whose viewer answers scripted requests with an “Access Denied” page; the project did not go round that check, and did not check the terms on which the images may be reproduced. Its only sight of the manuscript is the second page, opened once in a browser when the catalogue entry was made on 2 November 2026: no copy was kept, and the opening groups noted then agree with the first line of the transcription used here. The transcription itself is the contributor’s, made from the images with AI assistance.

02 The key

The cipher mixes numbers (10 to 114, and 241 and 242) with small letter-syllable signs, written here as ba, cle, je, fal, fol, xol and the like; these spellings are typographic approximations of the signs, not their values. A group may stand for a letter (25 = e, 28 = n, 32 = r), a syllable (fal = la, cla = ta, 54 = de, 73 = re) or a word (242 = tregua, 102 = avisareis, cel = effecto), and a value may have several groups (10 and 20 for a). Groups run on without word division.

Alberto Garvía García rebuilt the table from four letters of the same series that carry both the cipher and its clear text: I 242,42 (San Lorenzo, 21 February 1610), I 242,47 and I 242,48 (both of 20 May 1610) and I 242,33 (Lerma, 19 June 1610). He matched cipher sequences with the clear words and kept a reference for each value. A fifth letter, I 242,71 (Madrid, 20 February 1611), was kept apart as a negative control. The clear texts of those letters are decipherments of the time; nothing in them is claimed as new.

The table for this letter has 74 groups. Sixty-six are matched with a clear text. Eight have no sure value: the sign called U01 (read buena) and the numbers 101 (aviso), 114 (bien) and 241 (trato), which have no parallel and take their value from the context; e = mu, also from the context; fo = so, from a single parallel; and two looped signs written 28~ and 58~, taken as nulls. The key is therefore partial. The whole table is in the repository (key.tsv).

03 The checks

The project re-derived the reading from the contributor’s transcription and table with a parser of its own, which tries every way of cutting the digit runs: it gives his ten lines, connected Spanish from the first group to the last. All 211 groups of his own division have a value in the table and decode to his reading letter for letter. This shows that the table reads the transcription; it does not show that the transcription is right.

The transcription has outside support. Brice Dudout had transcribed the same leaf on his own (on 3 November 2026, by his record), and his digit strings agree with the contributor’s on every one of the ten lines. They differ on one sign of line 5, bo (which gives por) against 60; the contributor looked again at the images and keeps bo, and Dudout marks his own reading doubtful. The other differences are spellings of the same signs.

Dudout also sent his alignment of a further glossed letter of the family, I 243,18 (Lerma, 30 April 1610): 114 pairs of cipher group and gloss. Of the pairs whose group is in the table, 71 agree with it and one does not (a group glossed or once and er beside it, which looks like a slip of the alignment); 24 groups of that letter are not in the table. None of the open groups occurs in it, so it supports the table and closes nothing. The alignment was not compared with the image here. Dudout reports as well that a test he had declared for a table of the whole family, on I 242,42 held out, failed his own bar (0.640 against a bar of 0.85); that test is of his table, not of the one used here, and I 242,42 is one of the letters this key was built from.

The contributor’s control windows on the sibling letters (27 of 34 earlier ones agree, and 4 of 4 added later) are checks of a key against the passages it was built from, not blind tests. The project’s statistical check scores the reading at −1.948 per letter in a Spanish 4-gram model, against −3.627 on average for 200 random keys of the same structure (z 7.7) and −2.176 at best for a key fitted to the shuffled ciphertext (z 10.4): verdict pass, on 205 groups, with a key that came from the sibling letters and not from this text.

No earlier reading of the letter was found. The contributor searched S. Tomokiyo’s pages, DECODE, Retortillo Atienza (2019) and the Correia thesis, and a web search here found nothing. He opened Monumenta Missionária Africana, first series, vol. V, p. 587: it prints a different dispatch on Pinda, of 31 May 1610, not this letter. Devos, Les chiffres de Philippe II (1950), was identified and not seen by anyone.

04 The reading

De [buena] parte se ha tenido [aviso] que olandeses han ydo a asentar fatoria al [puerto] de Pinda en la costa de Angola y que allí y en [otras] partes van [entablando] su [trato] contra lo [capitulado] en la tregua, y [porque] no es [bien] pasar esto en [disimulación], os encargo y [mando] procuréis que se remedie por los medios que os pareciere, sin que vaya [persona] a este effecto a las Islas, pues no convendría que fuese por la reputación, y avisaréis me de lo que se hiciere.

Square brackets mark the words that are not sure. In English: “From a good quarter word has been had that Dutchmen have gone to set up a factory at the port of Pinda on the coast of Angola, and that there and in other parts they are entering upon their trade contrary to what was agreed in the truce; and because it is not right to let this pass in dissimulation, I charge and command you to see that it is remedied by whatever means seem fitting to you, without a person going to the Islands for this purpose, since it would not be fitting that he should go, for reputation’s sake; and you shall inform me of what is done.”

Tregua has its value from the sibling I 242,48; that it is the Twelve Years’ Truce is the contributor’s interpretation, and the text names no article. Islas is kept as deciphered: which islands are meant is not in the text. Fatoria is the spelling the groups give.

Counted by the README rule, strictly: the ten lines hold 89 words, and 77 are sure, which is 86.5%, under the 95% bar, so the extent is partial. Ten of the twelve holes are bracketed in the contributor’s own reading (the eight groups without a sure value, and otras, where a sign gives “otraas”); the other two, mando and porque, are words he reads as firm that stand on a sign in question. His own count is 78 of 88 words (88.6%). Read here as sentences against the translation, every clause of the order holds (grade A).

05 What remains uncertain

  • The transcription has not been compared with the manuscript by the project: the Memoria de Madrid viewer refuses scripted requests, and it needs a person at a browser. Two publication packages that the contributor attached to the issue as zip files could not be downloaded (403) and were not seen; nothing here rests on them.
  • buena (U01), aviso (101), trato (241), bien (114): codes with a value from the context only (grade I). disimulación: the sign e = mu, from the context (I). persona: fo = so, from a single parallel (M).
  • puerto, entablando, capitulado: each has a looped sign (28~ or 58~) inside it, taken as a null without a parallel (M). otras: the sign m = as gives “otraas”, not explained.
  • mando (line 6): the sign is printed with a query in the transcription; its value comes from a sibling (C on the value, the sign doubtful). porque (line 5): bo or 60, as above; por fits the sense.
  • An open question on the last sign of line 4, which gives the tu of capitulado: the contributor writes it clu, Brice Dudout writes it du, and in I 243,18 the sign Dudout writes du is glossed conde twice. Either these are two signs in two hands, or the value in line 4 needs a second look on the images. It has been asked of both and is not answered.
  • The open groups can still be worked: further letters of the series (I 242,66 with a gloss; I 242,35, 59 and 60 without) have not been transcribed by anyone, and a glossed one may carry them. Those letters, and the three other leaves without a decipherment, stay in the catalogue as entry 1988.
  • No human palaeographer has checked the signs, and the sibling clear texts behind the key values were not seen by the project.

## Translation

From a good quarter word has been had that Dutchmen have gone to set up a factory at the port of Pinda on the coast of Angola, and that there and in other parts they are entering upon their trade contrary to what was agreed in the truce; and because it is not right to let this pass in dissimulation, I charge and command you to see that it is remedied by whatever means seem fitting to you, without a person going to the Islands for this purpose, since it would not be fitting that he should go, for reputation's sake; and you shall inform me of what is done.

## Notes

- "Fatoria" is kept as deciphered (not modernised to "factoría"); "tregua" is read from the sibling I 242,48 (242 = tregua); that it is the Twelve Years' Truce is the contributor's contextual interpretation, the body names no article. "Islas" is kept as deciphered; reading it as the Dutch provinces is an interpretation. The group "je" ends "olandeses"; it does not license adding "se" before "han ydo".
- Open signs, by the contributor's own table: U01 (buena), 101 and 114 and 241 (contextual codes, no direct parallel), e = mu, fo = so (persona), the nulls 28~ and 58~, the anomaly m = as giving "otraas".
- The contributor's figures, as his (not the README read bar): 173 of 211 cipher-group occurrences (81.99%) belong to firmly readable words; 202 of 211 (95.73%) have an equivalence at table level; 78 of 88 words firm (88.6%); 82 of 88 if the grade-M values are accepted.
- The share recorded here is the project's strict recount of 14 Nov 2026 by the README rule: 89 words, 77 sure (86.5%). The holes are the ten bracketed words above and two words on a sign the transcriptions do not settle (*mando*, the sign printed gel{b?}; *porque* in line 5, bo or 60). It is under the 95% bar, and the transcription is unchecked against the images, so this is read in part (NOTES.md, Strict count).

## Editorial notes

- The translation is provisional and follows the line numbering of `reading.md`.
- Square brackets preserve unresolved readings and are not evidence that the proposed words have been deciphered.

- "Islands" translates *Islas* without assuming a specific geographical identification.
- No independent palaeographic verification is claimed.
